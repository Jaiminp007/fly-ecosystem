# Decoupled transfer steering diagnosis

The fixed plan in `DECOUPLED_TRANSFER_COMPONENT_PLAN.md` compares A, B and D
steering models on the same eight reused candidate D worlds and transfer probe
seeds. All three arms copy D taste and matching A motor banks and pay 326 model
writes. Positive contrasts favor the later steering model.

| Contrast | Energy/founder | Positive histories | Net intake/founder | Positive histories | Survivor difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| B minus A | +0.284710 | 6/8 | +0.050494 | 6/8 | 0 |
| D minus B | -0.357410 | 2/8 | -0.417050 | 1/8 | 0 |
| D minus A | -0.072700 | 4/8 | -0.366557 | 2/8 | 0 |

Each survivor difference is zero in every history. B steering improves the
mean transfer outcome over A on these worlds, while subsequent C/D experience
is associated with a large mean decline relative to B. This does not isolate
whether C or D caused the decline, establish an affordable retention policy,
or show that B steering beats the original reset and cheap controls under
matched costs. Outcomes include physical interactions and changed random
trajectories. The result is selected development evidence, not qualification.

Evidence: `evidence/decoupled-transfer-component/20261026-v1`. The checker
independently reconstructs 186 model assignments and their work charges,
checks 47,412 decision/receipt pairs and frozen weights, and exactly replays
24 captured-source arms. It does not independently simulate physics or prove
the physical adequacy of the energy model. Five focused tests pass.

Next distinguish B-to-C from C-to-D using the saved C steering model in the
same paid setup; this is another outcome-informed diagnostic. If the loss can
be localized, investigate an acquired, physically paid mechanism to preserve
beneficial behavior under later experience, and compare against reset and
cheap controls as well as retention and pressure gates. A post-hoc B checkpoint
or phase-dependent model choice is not itself a qualified autonomous skill.
