# Own-steering energy diagnosis

The independent factual census reconstructs 814,342 decision/receipt pairs
from the failed development study. It verifies trace hashes and the energy
balance including food, toxin, recorded cost, death energy and debt. It is an
outcome-selected diagnostic on reused histories, not a new performance test.

In the seven histories with a C checkpoint, frozen current learners take in
692.53 beneficial energy units versus 1,108.23 for reset, a deficit of 415.71.
Their recorded physical costs are 290.40 versus 429.19, a saving of 138.79.
Both figures include the complete surviving populations, not just survivors at
the end. The frozen current arm has 23 survivors versus 30 in reset. The
missing eighth history remains missing and the original joint gate stays failed.

Current takes 2,481 stationary, 1,659 half-speed and 2,495 full-speed actions;
reset takes 7,680 full-speed actions and no slower actions. The cheap native-full
control also takes 7,680 full-speed actions and records the same total intake as
reset in these seven histories. Different lifetimes affect these aggregate action
counts, so they alone do not prove speed caused the outcome. In acquisition,
learned bodies consume 4,601.92 beneficial units while incurring 4,495.50 toxin
damage, showing that harmful food is also a major pressure in this cue task.

Raw per-history counts, action/speed bins and balance reconstruction are in
`evidence/own-steering-energy-diagnosis/20261026-v1`. Source trace hashes and
the existing physical-accounting identity were checked; physical dynamics were
not independently simulated.

One fixed outcome-informed causal diagnostic follows: for each available C
anchor, retain the frozen current steering/taste/motor learner, but force actual
full-speed movement with the existing fixed-move interface. Compare against the
already captured current, reset and cheap native-full forks on the same world,
seed, controller and inherited debt. Capture all decisions, receipts and final
checkpoints; charge the ignored motor model, preserve all debt, and never use
these counterfactual outcomes as learner labels. This isolates a total
intervention in an interacting world, not a fixed-neighbor effect. No parameter
or threshold sweep is allowed, and a positive intervention would not establish
acquired retention, functional pressure, transfer qualification or RSI.

## Fixed-speed intervention result

All seven available frozen C forks were collected; the extinct eighth history
remains missing. Forcing full speed improved budget-adjusted energy relative to
the original current policy by +2.478643 per founder on average, positive in
7/7, and recovered six aggregate survivors. Against reset it gained +0.825853
on average and won 5/7 but lost one survivor. Against cheap native-full it gained
+0.711259 and won 5/7, also losing one survivor. These are descriptive effects
on reused, outcome-selected histories; missing data and the negative survivor
comparison mean the original transfer gate is not satisfied.

The result supports a causal role for the movement command in these interacting
worlds, not a proof that speed alone explains all failure or that frozen steering
is an acquired skill. Evidence and captured sources are in
`evidence/own-steering-speed-intervention/20261026-v1`. Independent readback
checks 7,482 decisions and receipts, actual full-speed outputs, frozen weights,
matched starting worlds, reported-work/storage/debt and comparisons. It does
not independently simulate physics or reconstruct every operation.
