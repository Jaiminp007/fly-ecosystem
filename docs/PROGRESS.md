# Plain-language progress tracker

The goal is flies that learn useful behavior, remember old skills while learning new ones, and eventually discover better ways to learn. The final step needs experiments showing that their improvement process itself gets better.

- **Done: working ecosystem and dashboard.** Worlds can run, save, restore and report energy, survival and learning state.
- **Done: audit of earlier evidence.** We found limits in earlier retention and improvement claims and preserved the corrections.
- **Done: action/history/reward diagnostic.** The experiment and its independent checks finished; its scientific gate failed.
- **Done: four fixed learning studies.** Original SARSA, tile-coded SARSA, bounded replay and a sparse reward-gated policy were tested and audited. All four failed their full qualification requirements. Passing software checks does not turn those failures into learning success.
- **Done: new smell/food environment.** Different external cues can identify beneficial and harmful food; bodies still pay real movement, metabolism and computation costs. Accounting and save/restore tests pass.
- **Done: check that smells and actions can help.** In 128 nonlearning worlds, knowing the smells beat fixed/random/shuffled controls and passed the null check. Both audits passed. This establishes an opportunity to learn, not that a fly learned it. Energy losses and many deaths remain.
- **Next: qualify learning and remembering.** Test one fixed learner on fresh histories, with learning-disabled, reset, cue-blind, shuffled and null controls, separate old-skill tests and original-environment transfer.
- **Still required: autonomous changes to the learning method.** At least two successive accepted revisions must improve behavior while preserving earlier skills.
- **Still required: prove a better improvement process.** Compare against copies with the same improved learner, facts and total budget. More knowledge or compute alone must not explain the gain.
- **Final claim: unachieved.** No recursive self-improvement has been demonstrated. The earlier roughly 35% estimate was a rough milestone estimate, not a measured completion score or promised deadline.

SARSA means a learner repeatedly asks: where am I, what will I do, what happened, where am I now, and what will I do next? Its usefulness depends on experimentally demonstrated outcomes. The smell-informed controller above was given the meanings for evaluation; it did not learn them.
