# Deterministic taste continuation

From the same saved end-A physical checkpoints, the fixed threshold arm uses
the body's learned taste estimate to accept when it exceeds 0.5; the matched
sample arm uses the original stochastic decision. Both compute the threshold
and consume and pay for a taste RNG draw. They do not use hidden valence or
counterfactual labels. Their RNG streams can diverge after different actions
and factual learning; only the starts are identical.

Across the eight reused long-B histories, threshold versus sample gives:

- Mean budget-adjusted B energy: **-0.144276 per founder**, with 2/8 positive
  histories; mean B net intake is **-0.392471 per founder**.
- Aggregate end-B survivors: **+6**, despite the lower mean energy.
- At the end of D, threshold has three survivors across three histories;
  sample has none. Missing or extinct histories do not pass any gate.

These are total-world developmental effects, not paired individual rescues or
evidence that an acquired threshold skill passes. The intervention does not
establish useful B acquisition, A/B pressure and retention, original-schedule
performance, transfer against strong controls or fresh qualification. Do not
promote a survivor-only subset or tune the threshold on these outcomes.

Ten focused tests pass. Captured source, matched starts, compressed traces and
results are in `evidence/deterministic-taste-continuation/20261026-v1`. The
independent readback checks 367,184 decision/receipt pairs, cue sensing,
recorded taste RNG/decisions, actual pending credit, encoded state and ledgers
across 38 exact source replays. This is not independent physical simulation or
a hardware-energy measurement. All previous failures and reserves remain.
