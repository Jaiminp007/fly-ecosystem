# A-retention component diagnosis

The outcome-selected motor-freeze continuation leaves A retention at 88.45%
against the 95% requirement. Its two motor banks are frozen after A, while taste
and steering continue learning. Test whether the same-D-body A-probe deficit is
associated with taste weights, steering weights or their interaction before
proposing another learner. This is a fixed post-outcome diagnosis on the eight
already used development histories, not fresh qualification.

For each surviving D body, first verify its two D motor-bank weights exactly
match its own A weights. From the same D physical world, RNG, debt, A-task seed
and 256-tick horizon, run four frozen model combinations: A taste/A steering,
A taste/D steering, D taste/A steering and D taste/D steering. Every arm uses
the same D motor banks and all other D state. Include the full reset model as a
strong reference. All five arms perform and pay for the same extra 27 taste and
steering weight-copy operations per live founder, in addition to their ordinary
fork/freeze/reset costs. No body is revived and no extra energy or food is added.

Save complete traces, source/input hashes, physical start hashes, initial and
final checkpoints, debt, outcomes and per-history comparisons. Independently
verify model provenance, frozen weights, action/receipt pairing, work/storage/
payment ledgers and aggregate arithmetic. The factorial contrast is a
total-world effect: policies change consumption, deaths and RNG trajectories.
It is not an isolated individual treatment or a counterfactual training label.
Do not tune based on this diagnostic or lower the original 95% retention,
survival, transfer, functional-pressure and fresh qualification requirements.
