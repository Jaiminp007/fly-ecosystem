steering models on each reused shadow and stop D world, under the original A
and B probe seeds. Contrasts are B minus A for the named component, in
budget-adjusted energy per founder; each pair holds the other component fixed.

On stop-world A probes the B taste replacement raises energy at A steering
in 6/8 histories, and raises net intake by +0.112820 per founder on average.
On B probes it raises energy at A steering in 7/8 histories and net intake by
+0.220285. The corresponding shadow-world mean taste changes are negative.
Interaction and survivor changes occur in some arms; effects are not safely
attributable to taste alone regardless of steering. Copy charges and evolved
worlds differ from the original unmodified probe comparison, so the table is
not a substitute for that study's pressure or transfer gates.

Evidence: `evidence/contact-stop-factorial/20261026-v1`. Five focused tests
pass. An independent checker reconstructs 1,056 charged model assignments,
verifies 258,802 decision/receipt pairs, reported work and debt, and exactly
replays all 128 arms. These are selected, outcome-informed development worlds;
different trajectories and shared bodies preclude individual-effect claims.
The audit does not independently simulate physics or establish low-level
pricing adequacy. No acquired-skill qualification or autonomous revision is
demonstrated; all previous failures and reserve boundaries remain intact.

The next causal question is whether the favorable stop-world A taste shift
arises from shared weights, cue-specific residuals, or their interaction. A
predeclared matched shared/residual factorial with saved A/B components and
fixed steering can distinguish their physically realized contributions; do not
infer that mechanism merely from the present taste/steering contrast.
