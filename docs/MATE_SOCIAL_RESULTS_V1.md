## Reading: selection has nothing to select on (and nothing to select for)

The result localizes the mating track's problem precisely, and it is
not transmission fidelity (V1 proved copies are exact), not
variation (V1b's mutants were neutral), and not selection pressure
(this line: copying the winner changes nothing). It is ENCOUNTERS:
gen-2 happens in 1 of 6 histories regardless of what's transmitted,
because compounding requires matings and matings are rare
(~0.05–0.15/main, spawn anti-qualified per ENCOUNTER_ESTIMATE).
Selection cannot act where no mating events occur, and with one event
per six histories there is nothing to select for.

This closes the obvious transmission-mechanism space: exact copy
(neutral), mutated copy (unconfirmed), best-copy (neutral). A future
mating line needs a different bottleneck to attack — encounter rate
(spawn/attraction/aggregation ecology), not transmission content.
Candidates already specified, none licensed here: quorum-gated
behavior, density-plus-attraction worlds, informed-minority
transplants, cost-gated horizontal copy (pay to copy a neighbor),
clumped-food mating systems (Emlen-Oring), carry-over worlds. The
line closes either way. No V2 without a new rationale (a new
bottleneck, not a new copy rule). Cumulative 7683 + 12 = 7695
(+12 ledgered; per-line authoritative). No RSI claim.
