# Bounded contiguous replay development

The previous fixed linear Expected SARSA screen failed its skill, retention, and survival requirements. The local tile representation also failed development. Those results and their unused confirmation reserves remain preserved. This study makes one fresh temporal-credit change while retaining the original 40 linear features, live learner, rates, buffer, environment schedule, endpoints, and nine distinct-task skill probes.

The candidate uniformly samples one matured transition from its 16 recent plus 48 reservoir records. It builds a forward correction over no more than 16 consecutive records from the same body. A continuation requires consecutive experience identifiers, matching decision times, and exact equality between the stored next and following state features. It stops at a terminal, missing record, gap, feature mismatch, or horizon. Each correction term is a current Expected SARSA TD error. The next-action coefficient is the discount times .9 times the smaller of one and the current epsilon-greedy action probability divided by the probability recorded when that action was actually selected. One gradient update is applied at the sampled anchor. No future outcome is attached to an earlier decision before it matures.

The matched one-step program has the same features, online updates, replay buffer, logged choice probabilities, and state/work accounting, with the replay continuation coefficient fixed to zero. The original linear program and fixed feeding reflex are additional controls. A history must have at least 10% multi-step replay updates for the candidate activity gate to pass. Memory use, operations, and embodied energy use the screen's existing declared prices. CPU and wall time are reported separately.

Retrace is motivated by a correction that can limit the effect of actions sampled under a different behavior policy. The cited paper establishes theoretical properties under its stated assumptions; this embodied controller uses finite replay, nonlinear closed-loop ecology, shared parameters, and no proof that those assumptions or guarantees apply. Here it is a fixed empirical hypothesis only. [Munos et al., “Safe and Efficient Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning” (NeurIPS 2016)](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2016/file/c3992e9a68c5ae12bd18488bc579b30d-Paper.pdf).

The frozen protocol uses eight fresh development histories and reserves sixteen additional fresh histories. It tests twelve required energy contrasts (including separate A/B initial-skill, acquisition, and retention tests), nine nonnegative mean-survival guards, and the activity gate in every history. Thirteen total comparisons share a Bonferroni family alpha of .05/13; the trace attribution is optional. The development gate requires all twelve required means to be positive, every survival guard to be nonnegative, and every history to pass the activity gate. Only that result plus passing cold physical and independent numeric audits can open confirmation. Confirmation uses the same unchanged candidate and requires positive simultaneous Student-t lower bounds and exact paired sign-flip p<=.05/13 for every required contrast, plus the same survival and activity gates.

The matched-control addition sets a declared maximum of 528 collection worlds and 503,808 world-steps: 224 development and 304 confirmation worlds, including the corresponding nine read-only probes. Audit replays are timed repeat computation, not extra scientific observations. The new protocol captures its complete source graph, prior protocol seed registries, test seed literals, source hashes, and both reserved and development seeds before outcomes. Any failed development gate leaves the confirmation directory unopened. Even a successful confirmation would not establish autonomous persistent algorithm revision, improved search, ecological readiness, or RSI.
