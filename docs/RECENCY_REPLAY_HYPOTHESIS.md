# Factual recency replay hypothesis

In the adjacent-opposed diagnostic, saved B taste models remain negatively
separated on the balanced B cues despite factual B training. The B-checkpoint
reservoirs of D survivors contain 1,257 A and 617 B records in observe, and
1,408 A and 701 B records in filter. These are selected, repeated records, not
a count of replay updates or proof of sampling bias. Per-founder A/B exposure
is highly uneven; the aggregate mean example counts cannot be used as if every
founder had the same ratio. The physical crossover also shows substantial
steering effects, so a taste-only remedy cannot be presumed sufficient.

A testable revision is a paid, bounded two-timescale taste replay: retain the
existing 32-entry factual reservoir plus eight most recent factual examples;
on each eligible update, replay one sampled example, selected half the time
from each store. A matched uniform-replay control must maintain the same two
stores, RNG state, candidate index calculations, and charged storage/work,
but replay only the reservoir candidate. Both must use only the body's own
stationary contact feedback, with no phase clock, hidden label, fabricated
outcome or altered number of gradient updates. The one-half choice and eight
entries are a single proposed design, not a sweep or a fitted threshold.

Before physical deployment, compare the two revisions offline on the same
reconstructed factual per-founder A/B/C traces. Prespecify paired B cue-score
and A retention diagnostics, retain all original histories and outcome-selected
bodies, independently reconstruct example provenance and RNG, and count the
extra work and storage even though an offline predictor does not pay physical
energy. Valences may be used only by the evaluator. An offline result cannot
show causal physical benefit or qualification; even a favorable signal requires
an equally paid physical study on both the original and conflicting curricula,
the unchanged pressure, survival and transfer gates, and fresh validation.
