# Cue-shared factual exposure and transfer decomposition

The fixed post-outcome census reconstructs actual decisions from the failed
development tapes; it does not change the failed gates. In the cue-shared arm,
all 266,221 A/B/C decisions had a present cue, while all 14,581 D decisions and
all 14,080 transfer decisions had no cue. Transfer uses full speed and accepts
food on every decision; 5,024 decisions execute midpoint steering. Reset would
choose full speed and native steering on those same recorded observations.

All 56 cue-shared D survivors had zero absent-cue residual weights at both A
and C checkpoints; all 56 changed that bank during D and actually executed
both native and midpoint steering there. This is outcome-selected survivor
support, not evidence that the actions had beneficial or causal effects. The
replay arm similarly observed cues throughout A/B/C and none in D/transfer;
its interacting trajectories and 68 D survivors differ.

On 11,757 transfer decisions where midpoint steering was available, the saved
cue-shared shared and absent-residual value margins had opposite signs 3,266
times. Removing the residual in an offline calculation would change 1,077 of
14,080 recorded commands; removing the shared component would change 2,189.
These are same-observation numerical comparisons, not physical interventions,
individual causal effects or labels for training. They motivate a separately
fixed, equally billed component-mask intervention before choosing a new
learner; they do not establish which component helps.

Hashed per-history and per-founder records, saved-weight checks and the
component census are in `evidence/cue-shared-exposure/20261026-v1`. They rely
on the already audited decision traces and saved checkpoints, not an
independent physical simulation. All prior failures and closed reserves remain
unchanged; no retained-skill, functional-pressure, transfer or RSI claim follows.
