# Factual motor reward census

The saved motor models change their preferred speed on identical factual
transfer observations, and a fixed full-speed intervention improves the seven
available current forks. These findings do not identify the training mechanism.

Use all eight already recorded learned-arm acquisition tapes only. Reconstruct
each body's actual next-decision budget-energy difference for its previous
executed speed, using the terminal observation for a death. Exclude an unfinished
last nonterminal decision rather than fabricating its target. Group actual
transitions by A/B/C, executed speed and pre-action contact. Include the actual
physical receipt's beneficial intake, toxin damage and cost; record terminal and
phase-boundary counts. The same founder's budget difference divided by 120 is
the existing motor reward. No hypothetical speed, other body's outcome, probe
result or altered physical trajectory becomes a label.

Reconstruct the original motor feature, next feature, action, terminal flag and
reward for every retained reservoir item in the saved A/B/C checkpoints and final
state; verify source/tape hashes and founder provenance. This confirms retained
records reflect actual transitions, not the correctness or causal effect of the
full TD/replay algorithm. Report per-history and pooled descriptive totals, not
only pooled averages. Different speeds are chosen in different states and by
different survivors, so conditional reward means are not causal estimates.

This is a post-outcome development diagnosis. It cannot qualify learning,
functional-pressure retention, original-ecology transfer or RSI, and it does
not open fresh or closed reserves. Any new learner needs its own fixed design,
cost audit and matched prospective controls.
