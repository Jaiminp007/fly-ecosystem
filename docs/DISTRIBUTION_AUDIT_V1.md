## Findings

1. **WIDTH is flat everywhere.** Ginis 0.018–0.191 across all 37
   arms (median ~0.10); Zipf slopes −0.05 to −0.35 (nowhere near
   −1); top-1 shares 0.011–0.056; lognormal sigmas 0.09–0.36
   (tight). Births per founder ≈ 1.0–1.4 in every birthy arm.
   There is NO breadth oligarchy: no arm concentrates offspring
   in few founders.
2. **The Pareto language is RETIRED.** R186 specified the
   falsifier (uniform-ish Ginis retire "oligarchy" to "variance")
   and the data triggers it decisively (max Gini 0.191 << 0.5;
   max |Zipf| 0.35 << 1). "Few lines, most births" (R186),
   "iron law of lineages" (R183-Michels) are WITHDRAWN as
   breadth claims. What stands: compounding = DEPTH (gen-2/3
   observed rarely), never WIDTH — and depth rests on thin data
   (gen-3 once ever; gen-2 counts single-digit), disclosed, not
   upgraded.
3. **R190 growth-test UNANSWERABLE.** Proportionate (Gibrat) vs
   super-proportionate (Yule/Barabasi) needs per-founder
   birth-order/timing; frozen rows carry tallies only. The
   theories disagree about data that cannot judge them — closed
   as specified-unanswerable, no new worlds spent finding out.
   The lognormal-vs-Pareto bulk question is answered by (1)
   instead: NEITHER (too flat for Pareto, too narrow for
   lognormal — the bulk is approximately EQUAL, not skewed).
4. **Underdispersion, unexplained.** All 37 arms sit BELOW
   Poisson-equal (e.g., social-copyalways: 56 births/55 founders,
   Gini 0.018 — nearly every founder exactly one offspring).
   Candidate mechanisms (mating-energy gating, one-birth caps,
   bar throttling) are NOT tested here — flagged, not
   interpreted. The equality is real; its cause is unknown.
