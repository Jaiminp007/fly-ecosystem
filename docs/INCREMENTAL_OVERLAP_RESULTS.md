# Single-traversal overlap index: engineering result

The outcome-informed incremental index reproduces the original strict-tie
recommendation on 5,000 randomized geometries, boundary/duplicate/unsafe-cell
fixtures and all 83,617 replayed C/transfer decisions. All 16 captured-source
physical replay checkpoints match because the candidate was an offline observer,
not the controlling program.

Under the candidate's declared abstract scalar schedule and retained scratch
byte-time, the same-state cost estimate is lower by 9.819685 energy across C
and 2.158365 across transfer, or approximately 0.0511 and 0.0112 per original
founder. Its additional scratch storage is 27,445,661 and 6,553,699 byte-ticks;
44,267/67,348 C decisions and 10,669/16,269 transfer decisions are individually
cheaper. This is not a physical outcome: changed costs can change behavior,
competition and survival. It also depends on a declared operation convention,
not an independent machine-level energy measurement.

A separate post-outcome checker independently reconstructs the combinatorial
work and encoded scratch byte-time for all 83,617 decisions. Captured protocol,
sources, per-history results and readback are in
`evidence/incremental-overlap-measurement/20261026-v1`.

The selector is now an opt-in `spatialSelector: 'incremental'` in
`OdorRoutedExperiment`; the default and older checkpoints use `filtered`.
Sixteen focused tests pass for the new selector and existing routed/replay
paths, including same-world initial outputs, dynamically charged index bytes,
exact checkpoint continuation, matched fork starts and terminal debt. These
are engineering checks, not a complete embodied ledger audit or research
performance test. The original indexed failure remains preserved, and none of
the acquired skill, retention, transfer, functional-pressure, fresh
qualification or autonomous-revision requirements is met.
